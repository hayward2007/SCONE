\begin{abstract}
Mobile robots designed for efficient motion on flat ground often lose mobility on
stairs, whereas articulated legged robots can negotiate obstacles at the cost of
greater control complexity and slower transport. This paper studies whether one
distal mechanism can support both operating regimes without a separate wheel-leg
transformation mechanism. \robotname{} is a six-legged robot whose eighteen
actuators include six arc-shaped distal links that function as feet during
articulated walking, as quasi-wheels during fast flat-ground locomotion, and as
edge-engagement elements during stair climbing. We describe the mechanism,
mode-transition controller, and an experimental protocol covering untethered
operation, walking, quasi-rolling, continuous stair ascent, and irregular terrain.
