\section{RELATED WORK AND PROBLEM FORMULATION}

\subsection{Rotating-Leg and Hybrid Hexapods}

RHex demonstrates that six continuously rotating compliant legs can yield robust
mobility with a mechanically simple, open-loop gait
\cite{saranli2001rhex}. Q-Whex extends the quasi-wheeled rotating-leg design space
\cite{zhang2023qwhex}. At the other end of the spectrum, Cassino Hexapod III uses
articulated legs with omni-wheels at their distal ends to combine legged and
wheeled motion \cite{tedeschi2017cassino}. These systems establish that rotating
legs, articulated legs, and distal wheels each provide useful mobility, but they
make different choices about contact geometry, actuation, and control authority.

\subsection{Problem Definition}

Let the commanded locomotion mode be
$m \in \{\mathrm{walk},\mathrm{roll},\mathrm{climb}\}$. The design objective is
to realize all three modes with one distal mechanism and no hardware
reconfiguration. Let $v$ denote forward speed, $p_s$ traversal success rate,
$E_d$ electrical energy per traveled distance, and $h_s$ stair riser height. The
experimental question is not whether each mode can be demonstrated in isolation,
but whether the shared mechanism produces a useful operating envelope:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{O} = \{v_{\mathrm{walk}}, v_{\mathrm{roll}}, p_s(h_s), E_d,
  t_{\mathrm{transition}}\}.
  \label{eq:operating-envelope}
\end{equation}

The proposed design is considered successful only if (i) rolling is measurably
faster or more energy-efficient than walking on a common surface, (ii) the robot
ascends the target staircase repeatedly under onboard power, and (iii) its full
articulation provides a measurable stair-climbing advantage over a restricted
distal-only condition.
