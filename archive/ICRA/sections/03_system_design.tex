first leg stage, and IDs 13--18 actuate the distal stage. Table~\ref{tab:hardware}
records the hardware mapping that must remain identical between the physical

\subsection{Arc-Shaped Distal Link}

The distal link combines a rigid frame with a compliant TPU tread. Its geometry
must support three contact regimes: localized support during walking, sequential
arc contact during quasi-rolling, and edge engagement during stair ascent.
Figure~\ref{fig:arc-geometry} will define the arc radius $r$, opening angle

\subsection{Design Evolution}

The first prototype exposed four coupled limitations: a long parallel-link load
path, off-axis distal contact, insufficient tire friction, and inadequate
untethered power delivery. The current prototype removes the parallel mechanism,
aligns the distal contact with the leg plane, adds a TPU tread, and uses higher
torque actuators and an onboard battery system. This evolution motivates the
current design but is not, by itself, a controlled ablation because several
variables changed simultaneously.
