\section{EXPERIMENTAL METHOD}

\subsection{Reproducible Simulation Protocol}

Experiments use MuJoCo \cite{todorov2012mujoco} with $2\,$ms integration and
$20\,$ms control. Every trial creates a fresh floating-base model and controller.
The nominal flat test settles for $1\,$s and measures $6\,$s at
$\vect{c}=[0.18,0,0]$. The three same-model restrictions are:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
 \item[A] articulated tripod walking, distal joints in position mode with the
 continuous rolling term of (\ref{eq:superpose}) removed;
 \item[B] distal-only rotation, distal joints in velocity mode driven by
 (\ref{eq:rollcmd}) while the body and proximal targets are held at the staggered
 stance pose;
 \item[C] the full controller of Sec.~\ref{sub:superpose}, in which both terms of
 (\ref{eq:superpose}) are active simultaneously.
\end{itemize}
The restrictions therefore isolate control authority on one unchanged model. Two
caveats follow from Sec.~\ref{sub:tripod} and are stated before the results
rather than after them: A and C do not share the walking parameter set
($1.0\,$Hz, $D=0.50$, $90\,$mm stroke versus $0.8\,$Hz, $D=0.58$, $55\,$mm
stroke), and the rolling rate of (\ref{eq:rollcmd}) is scaled by normalized
command activity rather than tuned per condition. Neither condition was
separately optimized for maximum speed, so the comparison establishes the
qualitative ordering of the three regimes on one model and not the best speed
attainable by any of them.

The stair set contains three 3-step profiles: $(h,d)=(0.10,0.24)$,
$(0.15,0.20)$, and $(0.20,0.35)\,$m, where $d$ is the minimum tread. Strategies
are distal-only rotation, which holds every non-arc joint and spins the six arcs
at one constant rate so that no articulation is available; a synchronized
open-loop roller with a fixed front brace; and the full geometry-conditioned
controller. Reaching the upper landing defines success.
Transition trials measure the time from the first mode-change command until all
guarded actuator conditions are satisfied, followed by $2\,$s recovery.
Uneven-terrain smoke tests use three randomized seeds and $2\,$s windows; they
check execution, not publication-grade robustness.

\subsection{Metrics}

Body-frame velocity tracking error and integrated slip are
\begin{align}
 e_v&=\sqrt{\frac1T\int_0^T\|\vect{v}_{xy}(t)-\vect{c}_{xy}\|_2^2dt}, \label{eq:rmse}\\
 D_{\rm slip}&=\int_0^T\frac1{|\mathcal{K}(t)|}
 \sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}(t)}\|\vect{v}_{c,k}^{\parallel}(t)\|_2dt,     \label{eq:slip}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{K}$ contains loaded arc--ground contacts carrying more than
$1\,$N, about $2.5\%$ of the modeled body weight. Because $e_v$ is measured
against a constant command, a controller that overshoots that command is
penalized; $\bar v_x$ and $e_v$ are therefore read together. Absolute mechanical
work and mechanical cost of transport are
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm abs}=\int_0^T\sum_{j=1}^{18}|\tau_j\dot q_j|dt,
 \qquad C_{\rm mt}=\frac{W_{\rm abs}}{mgd_{xy}},                \label{eq:cot}
\end{equation}
with $d_{xy}$ the net planar displacement. An electrical estimate uses
$I_j=(V_j-K_j\dot q_j)/R_j$ and $E_e=\int\sum_j|V_jI_j|dt$. Because neither
battery conversion loss nor servo electronics is modeled, $E_e$ is diagnostic,
not physical battery energy. Uprightness is
$u_z=\vect{e}_z^\top R_{WB}\vect{e}_z$; termination occurs below $\cos60^\circ$
or on non-finite state.

\subsection{Scope of the Evidence}

Every trial writes one record carrying the source revision, the seed, and all
model scale factors, so each reported number is traceable to the configuration
that produced it. The nominal flat, stair, and transition conditions are single
deterministic trials and the uneven-terrain condition uses three seeds; they
support reproduction and ablation ordering, not confidence intervals. No
physical measurement enters any table, figure, or conclusion in this paper.
