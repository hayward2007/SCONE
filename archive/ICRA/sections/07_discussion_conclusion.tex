\section{DISCUSSION}

The discussion should distinguish demonstrated capability from causal evidence.
The historical v1--v2 comparison documents an engineering evolution, whereas the
same-hardware restrictions isolate the effect of the shared arc-leg morphology.
Interpret results relative to RHex-like rotating legs, articulated hexapods, and
distal-wheel hexapods without claiming that hybrid locomotion itself is new.

Expected limitations include open-loop dependence on stair geometry, limited
ground clearance in quasi-rolling mode, actuator thermal limits, and sensitivity
to tread friction. Claims about ``most stairs,'' outdoor robustness, payload, or
energy efficiency must be bounded by the actual tested range.

\section{CONCLUSION}

This paper studies a hexapod morphology in which one actuated arc-shaped distal
link serves as a walking foot, quasi-wheel, and stair-edge engagement mechanism.
The final conclusion will state only the verified operating envelope and the
measured trade-offs among the three modes.
